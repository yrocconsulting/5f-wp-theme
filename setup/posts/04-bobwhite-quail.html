That clear, two-note whistle, bob-WHITE!, is one of the most recognizable sounds in rural Texas. The northern bobwhite is a native quail with a devoted following of bird hunters, dog handlers and landowners who work to keep good quail country on the ground.

Know the Bird

The northern bobwhite (Colinus virginianus) is a small, round, reddish-brown quail about the size of a softball. Males have a bold white throat and eye stripe; females have the same pattern in buff. In fall and winter, bobwhites gather in coveys of roughly a dozen birds that feed, roost and flush together.

What Quail Need

  • Bunchgrass for nesting. Native bunchgrasses such as little bluestem give hens overhead cover for nests on the ground.
  • Forbs and bare ground for chicks. Young quail need open ground with weeds and wildflowers that attract the insects they eat.
  • Brushy cover nearby. Low, woody cover like plum thickets and shrubs gives coveys protection from hawks and weather.
  • Rain at the right time. Quail numbers swing with rainfall. A wet spring and summer can produce a boom year, and drought can do the opposite.

Across much of their range, bobwhite numbers have declined over the long term, mostly from habitat loss. Thoughtful land management such as prescribed grazing, rested ground and leaving brushy edges makes a real difference.

Hunting Tips

  • Hunt with dogs. Pointing and flushing dogs find and hold coveys that would otherwise run or sit tight unseen.
  • Pick a bird. When a covey explodes, choose one bird and stay on it instead of shooting into the group.
  • Leave seed for next year. Many quail hunters stop shooting a covey once it's down to about eight birds so it can survive the winter.
  • Wear blaze orange. Upland hunting means moving through cover with other hunters, so be visible and keep safe shooting lanes.

Before You Go

Texas quail season generally runs from late fall through winter. Season dates and daily bag limits are set each year, so check the current Texas Parks & Wildlife Outdoor Annual before you hunt.
