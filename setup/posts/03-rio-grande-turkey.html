Few sounds in the outdoors match a gobble rolling across a creek bottom at first light. Texas has the largest wild turkey population in the country, and most of those birds are Rio Grande turkeys, a native subspecies at home in the brushy, broken country of Central and North Texas.

Know the Bird

The Rio Grande wild turkey (Meleagris gallopavo intermedia) is a large, long-legged bird with coppery, iridescent feathers and buff-tipped tail feathers. Adult males, called gobblers or toms, have a red-and-blue head, a fleshy wattle and a "beard" of hair-like feathers hanging from the chest. Hens are smaller and duller in color for camouflage on the nest.

Daily Routine

  • Roost high near water. Rio Grandes roost overnight in tall trees, often pecans, cottonwoods and oaks along creeks and rivers, and may use the same roost for years.
  • Fly down at dawn. Birds gobble from the roost before flying down to open areas to feed and strut.
  • Feed on the move. Turkeys eat seeds, green shoots, insects, acorns and berries, covering a lot of ground during the day.
  • Return in the evening. Flocks head back toward the roost before sunset.

Hunting Tips

  • Roost them the night before. Listen at dusk for gobbles or the sound of birds flying up, then set up a respectful distance away before daylight.
  • Call less than you think. A few soft yelps, clucks and purrs are often enough. Let the gobbler do the work.
  • Total camouflage, total stillness. Turkeys have outstanding eyesight. Sit against a tree wider than your shoulders and don't move until it's time to shoot.
  • Aim for the head and neck. With a shotgun, wait for the bird to stretch its neck out of strut and keep shots within your pattern's effective range.

Before You Go

Texas has spring seasons for turkey across much of the state and fall seasons in some counties, with dates, bag limits and legal birds set by county and region. Check the current Texas Parks & Wildlife Outdoor Annual before you hunt.
