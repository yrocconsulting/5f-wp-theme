No animal is more tied to Texas hunting than the white-tailed deer. The state supports the largest whitetail population in the country, and each fall deer season brings families and friends back to the same blinds year after year.

Know the Animal

The white-tailed deer (Odocoileus virginianus) is named for the bright white underside of its tail, which it raises as a "flag" when alarmed. Bucks grow a new set of antlers every year, shedding them in late winter, and their antlers harden and lose their velvet in late summer.

Whitetails are most active at dawn and dusk. They have an excellent sense of smell, sharp hearing and eyes tuned to catch movement, so wind direction and stillness matter more than anything else on a deer hunt.

Where They Live on a North Texas Ranch

  • Creek bottoms and timber. Wooded drainages, like the bottoms along Big Sandy Creek at 5F Ranch, give deer cover and safe travel routes between bedding and feeding areas.
  • Edges. Deer love the transitions where timber meets pasture, especially along tree lines and fence rows.
  • Browse, forbs and acorns. Whitetails are browsers rather than grazers. They eat leaves, twigs, weeds and wildflowers, and they key in on acorns when oaks drop them in the fall.
  • Water. Ponds and creeks are especially important during hot, dry stretches.

Hunting Tips

  • Play the wind. Always set up with the wind blowing from where you expect deer toward you, never the other way around.
  • Hunt the rut. In North Texas, the rut typically peaks in November. Bucks move more and let their guard down while searching for does.
  • Be patient. Get to your stand early, stay still and give the woods time to settle.
  • Take ethical shots. Wait for a broadside or quartering-away deer and aim for the heart and lungs just behind the front shoulder.

Before You Go

Texas deer regulations are set county by county, including season dates, bag limits and antler restrictions in many counties. Hunters born on or after September 2, 1971 must complete a hunter education course, and harvested deer must be tagged and logged. Check the current Texas Parks & Wildlife Outdoor Annual for the county you're hunting before every season.
