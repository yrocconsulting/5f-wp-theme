Most of the animals in Field Notes are native Texas game. Feral hogs are the exception. They're an invasive species, and keeping their numbers down is one of the most important jobs on any Texas ranch, including ours. For hunters, that means opportunity all year long.

Where They Came From

Texas's feral hogs descend from domestic pigs brought over by early Spanish explorers and settlers, later mixed with Eurasian wild boar released for sport hunting. Today Texas has more feral hogs than any other state, with estimates in the millions, and they're found in nearly every county.

Why They're a Problem

  • They tear up the land. Hogs root through pastures, crops and food plots, leaving ground that looks like it's been plowed.
  • They foul water. Wallowing muddies ponds, stock tanks and creek banks.
  • They compete with native wildlife. Hogs eat the same acorns deer and turkey depend on, and they raid the nests of ground-nesting birds like quail and turkey.
  • They multiply fast. Sows can start breeding before they're a year old and can raise more than one litter a year, so populations rebound quickly.

Why Night Hunting Works

Hogs can be active any time, but heat and hunting pressure push them to move mostly after dark. That's where thermal optics come in. A thermal scope picks up body heat, so you can find hogs in a pitch-black field, see the whole sounder, and pick out your target before you shoot.

Hunting Tips

  • Hunt the sign. Look for fresh rooting, wallows, rubs on trees and trails along creek bottoms and field edges.
  • Mind the wind. Hogs have poor eyesight but an outstanding nose. Approach from downwind.
  • Know your shot. A hog's vitals sit low and forward, tight behind the front leg. Big boars also carry a tough "shield" of gristle over the shoulders, so use enough gun and good bullets.
  • Identify your target and what's beyond it. At night this matters even more. Never shoot at heat you haven't positively identified.

From the Field to the Table

Wild pork is lean and good eating, especially from younger hogs. Hogs can carry diseases that affect people, so wear gloves when field dressing and cook the meat thoroughly, to an internal temperature of at least 160°F.

Before You Go

Feral hogs are not a game animal in Texas, so there's no closed season or bag limit. Rules on licensing and on hunting at night can still apply depending on where and how you hunt, so check the current Texas Parks & Wildlife Outdoor Annual, and always hunt with the landowner's permission.
