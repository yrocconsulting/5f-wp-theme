When the first cool front pushes through in September, Texas hunters head for sunflower fields and stock tanks. The mourning dove opens the season for hundreds of thousands of hunters across the state, and it's one of the most widespread and abundant game birds in North America.

Know the Bird

The mourning dove (Zenaida macroura) is a slender, gray-brown bird about a foot long, with a small head, black spots on its wings and a long, pointed tail edged in white. Its soft, mournful coo-OO-oo-oo gives the bird its name, and its wings make a sharp whistle when it flushes.

Doves are built for speed. They twist, dive and change direction without warning, which is exactly what makes them such a challenge, and so much fun, to shoot.

What Doves Need

  • Seeds on bare ground. Doves feed almost entirely on seeds: sunflower, croton (dove weed), native grasses and waste grain. They prefer to pick from open, bare soil, which is why freshly shredded or disked strips draw birds.
  • Water every day. Doves usually visit water in the late afternoon, favoring ponds and tanks with open, gently sloping banks.
  • Roost and loafing trees. Dead limbs, power lines and tree lines near food give doves a place to rest and watch.
  • Grit. Like many seed-eaters, doves pick up small gravel to help grind their food.

Hunting Tips

  • Scout the flight lines. Doves travel the same routes between roost, food and water. Set up along those routes, not just in the middle of the field.
  • Sit still and blend in. Use natural cover such as a fence row, brush or tree line, and wear muted colors. Doves flare at movement.
  • Mark your birds. Downed doves vanish in stubble and weeds. Watch where each bird falls and pick it up before shooting again.
  • Bring plenty of shells and water. Early-season afternoons in Texas are hot, and doves make most hunters miss more than they'd like to admit.

Before You Go

Doves are migratory game birds, so federal and state rules both apply. In Texas you'll need a hunting license with a Migratory Game Bird Endorsement and HIP certification, and your shotgun must be plugged to hold no more than three shells. Season dates, zones and daily bag limits change from year to year, so always check the current Texas Parks & Wildlife Outdoor Annual before you hunt.
